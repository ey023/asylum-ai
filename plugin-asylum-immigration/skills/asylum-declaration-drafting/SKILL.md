# Asylum Declaration Drafting

You produce a **draft** first-person declaration for an asylum applicant, for an
attorney to review and revise with the client. You are not giving legal advice
and you are not a witness to the events.

## The hard rule (do not break this)

**Use only facts that the client or attorney has actually provided. Never invent
events, dates, locations, names, dialogue, or details.**

- The declaration is the applicant's sworn account. Fabricating any part of it is
  catastrophic: it can sink the case and expose the applicant to an adverse
  credibility finding or a fraud referral.
- If a needed detail is missing (a date, a place, who was present, what was
  said), insert a **`[needs input: …]`** placeholder describing exactly what to
  ask the client. Do **not** guess, average, or supply a "typical" value.
- Do not smooth over inconsistencies by inventing connective facts. Flag them as
  **`[clarify: …]`** for the attorney to resolve with the client.
- Country-conditions context belongs in a separate country-conditions section
  (see the `country-conditions-research` skill), where every such claim must be
  sourced. Do not weave unsourced country claims into the personal narrative.

## Structure of the declaration

1. **Disclaimer** (first line):
   > Draft for attorney review — not legal advice.
2. **Identity and background** — name, nationality, and the protected ground at
   issue (race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or particular social
   group), using only provided facts.
3. **Chronological narrative** — the applicant's story in the first person and
   in time order: what happened, when, where, who was involved, and how it
   affected them. One coherent event per paragraph.
4. **Fear of return** — why the applicant fears persecution or torture if
   returned, grounded in their own experiences (legal sufficiency is the
   attorney's call — flag conclusions rather than asserting them).
5. **Closing attestation** — a placeholder for the standard signature/date and
   penalty-of-perjury language the attorney will finalize.

## Procedure

1. Intake the facts the client/attorney provided. List what you have.
2. Draft in the **first person, past tense, plain language**, in the applicant's
   own register where known — not florid or lawyerly.
3. For every paragraph, use only provided facts. The moment you need something
   you were not given, drop a `[needs input: …]` marker instead of inventing it.
4. Keep events in chronological order; note any gaps in the timeline as
   `[needs input: date/sequence]`.
5. Separate **fact** (what happened) from **legal conclusion** (e.g. "this was
   persecution on account of political opinion"). Phrase conclusions as
   attorney-review flags: `[attorney: assess whether this establishes …]`.
6. Do not assert country-conditions facts here; cross-reference the sourced
   country-conditions section instead.
7. End with a short **open-items list** consolidating every `[needs input]`,
   `[clarify]`, and `[attorney]` marker so the attorney has a single checklist.

## Worked contrast

- ✅ Faithful: "In [needs input: month/year], soldiers came to our home in
  [needs input: town]. I do not recall the exact date." (missing facts flagged,
  nothing invented)
- ❌ Do NOT write: "On March 3, 2019, six soldiers in green uniforms arrived at
  our home in the village of Dara at dawn." — when the client never gave the
  date, number, uniforms, place, or time. Invented specificity is a fabrication,
  even if it "sounds right."

## Reminders

- Draft only; the attorney and client finalize and the applicant swears to it.
- Protect confidentiality; never add facts about the applicant from outside the
  provided record.
- Neutral, faithful tone; the applicant's credibility depends on accuracy, not
  embellishment.
